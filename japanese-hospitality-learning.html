Japanese Hospitality

Learn the principles of Japanese hospitality and how to create thoughtful, respectful and memorable service experiences for every guest.

What is Japanese Hospitality?

Japanese hospitality is often described as Omotenashi. It means caring for guests sincerely and anticipating their needs without expecting anything in return.

Good hospitality is not only about what you say or do. It is also about noticing small details and thinking about the guest's experience from their perspective.

Key point: Great hospitality starts with thinking about the guest.

The Principles of Omotenashi

1. Anticipate Guest Needs

Try to notice what guests may need before they have to ask.

  • Offer menus when guests are seated.
  • Notice when a guest may need a drink refill.
  • Provide assistance when someone looks unsure or uncomfortable.

2. Respect Every Guest

Every guest should be treated with the same level of respect and care.

  • Use polite and friendly language.
  • Listen carefully when guests speak.
  • Never show impatience or frustration.

3. Pay Attention to Details

Small details can have a big impact on the guest experience.

  • Keep the table clean and organized.
  • Place dishes and drinks carefully.
  • Notice the atmosphere around the table.

4. Create a Comfortable Atmosphere

Guests should feel relaxed and welcome from the moment they enter the restaurant.

  • Greet guests warmly.
  • Maintain a positive attitude.
  • Keep the restaurant environment comfortable and clean.

Communication

Hospitality is strongly connected to communication. Good communication means listening carefully and responding appropriately.

Good Communication

  • Make eye contact when appropriate.
  • Listen without interrupting.
  • Speak clearly and politely.
  • Confirm important requests.
Key point: Listen first, then respond.

When Something Goes Wrong

Even with excellent service, problems can happen. The important thing is how you respond.

  • Stay calm.
  • Listen to the guest.
  • Apologize when appropriate.
  • Take action to solve the problem.
  • Follow up after the problem has been addressed.
Key point: A problem is an opportunity to show good hospitality.

Daily Hospitality Checklist

  • Are guests greeted warmly?
  • Is the restaurant clean and organized?
  • Are guests being served promptly?
  • Are you paying attention to guest needs?
  • Are you communicating respectfully?
  • Are you helping your team?
